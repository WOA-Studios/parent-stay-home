A difficult outcome

The Outcome Was Not What We Hoped

You do not have to handle every administrative task immediately. Start with the next necessary step.

Right now

  • Write down who notified you, their callback number, and where your parent is now.
  • Ask what immediate decision, if any, is required from you.
  • Identify one trusted person who can help you keep track of calls, names, and next steps.
  • If you are traveling, write down who is handling local matters until you arrive.
  • Keep important papers, the binder, and incoming documents together.

As the first hours and days unfold

  • Follow instructions from the hospital, medical examiner/coroner, funeral home, or local authority involved.
  • Ask how and when certified death certificates will be available; many later tasks require certified copies.
  • Keep a list of agencies, financial institutions, insurers, utilities, memberships, and other organizations that will eventually need notification.
  • Do not assume every account or payment should be changed immediately; rules and authority can vary.
  • Use state-specific legal guidance when estate, property, probate, or authority questions arise.
This page is not legal or financial advice.Procedures after a death vary by state and circumstance. USAGov provides current federal guidance on death certificates, government notifications, Social Security/Medicare, veterans benefits, and other common tasks.
