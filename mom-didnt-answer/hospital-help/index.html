Parent is being transported

They’re Going to the Hospital

Capture the information coming toward you first. Then decide what you need to do next.

First: write down what you were told

Who called me?
Callback number
What did they say?
Hospital / destination — unknown or known?
Who gave me the destination?
Estimated transport / departure time
Estimated arrival, if known
Local person following / meeting them

Then: gather what may help

  • Parent’s full name and date of birth
  • Medication list and known allergies, if available
  • Primary doctor and pharmacy information
  • Insurance information, if available
  • Health-care agent or advance directive information, if applicable and available
  • Recent hospitalization, procedure, illness, fall, or medication change dates
  • A way to reach the local person who is with or near your parent

If discharge planning begins

  • Ask what care or supervision will be needed at home.
  • Ask what medications changed and what follow-up appointments are needed.
  • Ask whether equipment, home health, therapy, transportation, or other services are being recommended.
  • Ask what warning signs should prompt a call to the care team or emergency services.
  • Make sure the person who will actually provide care understands the plan.

Medicare publishes a discharge-planning checklist for patients and caregivers preparing to leave a hospital, nursing home, or other care setting. This page is organizational support and does not replace the discharge instructions from your parent’s care team.
