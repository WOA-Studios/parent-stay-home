Response ladder

Mom or Dad didn’t answer

The useful question is not only “Did they answer?” It is “Is this different from what is normal for them?”

1

Try the usual ways you communicate

Call again, text, or use the method they normally respond to.

2

Ask whether this is unusual

Think about their normal callback pattern and any recent illness, fall, hospitalization, procedure, or unusual conversation.

3

Activate the backup layer

Call the people who have actually agreed to respond: primary local responder, Backup #1, then Backup #2.

4

If nobody can physically verify safety

Use the local non-emergency or public-safety contact you identified in advance and explain the situation. Procedures vary by community.

5

If immediate emergency assistance may be needed

Use the emergency system for your parent’s location. Be ready with the exact address, callback number, what you know, and legitimate access information.

When they answer, but...

Something feels off

  • What sounded, looked, or felt different?
  • When did you first notice it?
  • Recent illness or fall? Note the date if known.
  • Recent hospitalization, procedure, or medication change?
  • Has another caregiver, neighbor, or family member noticed something?
  • Has something similar happened before? Check the binder’s Incident Index.

Help is on the way

Gather what you know

You may not know what is wrong yet. Share facts, not guesses.

  • Parent’s exact address and apartment/unit
  • Your callback number
  • Alternate family contact number
  • Who is at the residence, if anyone
  • Who can provide legitimate home access
  • Where the Emergency Information Sheet is kept
  • Why you are concerned — including “I do not yet know what is wrong”
