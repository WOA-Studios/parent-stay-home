When a missed call feels different from normal

What to do when your aging parent isn’t answering the phone

A missed call is not always an emergency. But when your parent usually answers, calls back, or follows a predictable routine, silence can feel different. The goal is to respond calmly, notice what is unusual, and move one step at a time.

Start with what you know

Ask one question first: Is this unusual for your parent?

Think about what normally happens. Do they usually answer after one or two calls? Do they call back within an hour? Are they often at appointments, church, a senior center, outside, or taking a nap at this time?

The concern rises when the missed contact is different from their normal routine, especially if there has been a recent illness, fall, hospitalization, procedure, medication change, or an earlier conversation that worried you.

If you believe there may be an immediate emergency: seek emergency assistance for your parent’s location. If you are calling from another city or state, 911.gov says that 911 calls generally cannot be transferred to another jurisdiction; the best option is usually the local 10-digit law-enforcement number for the community where help is needed.

A calm response ladder

Work through these steps in order.

1. Try the usual ways you communicate

Call again. Send a text. Try the messaging app, landline, video call, or other method your parent normally responds to.

A single unanswered call by itself may not mean anything has happened.

2. Check the normal routine

Consider where your parent may reasonably be and whether this timing fits their usual day. If you know about an appointment, activity, visitor, or transportation schedule, check that first.

3. Contact the people who have agreed to help

Call the local person your parent has approved as a backup: a relative, friend, neighbor, building staff member, caregiver, or other trusted person who has actually agreed to respond.

Do not assume proximity means availability. A useful plan names specific people and backups in advance.

4. Think about home access

If someone is going to the home, can they get inside legally and safely? Know who has a key, lockbox information, building access, or other authorized entry method.

5. If nobody can verify your parent’s safety

Use the local public-safety or non-emergency contact you identified for your parent’s community and explain why you are concerned. Procedures vary by location.

6. If the situation may be an emergency

Seek emergency assistance for your parent’s location. Be ready with the exact address, apartment or unit number, your callback number, what you know, and any legitimate access information.

What to have ready

These details can make the next call easier.

Exact location

Street address, apartment or unit number, building name, gate information, and any details responders may need to find the residence.

Callback information

Your phone number plus another family contact if one is available.

Local backup contacts

Names and numbers for people who have agreed to check on your parent or help with access.

Critical health information

Current medications, allergies, doctors, recent hospitalizations, and other information that may matter if your parent is taken for medical care.

If your parent answers, but something feels off

Capture observations, not diagnoses.

You may notice confusion, unusual weakness, slurred or difficult speech, a recent fall, a medication change, or simply that your parent sounds unlike themselves. Write down what you actually observe and when you noticed it.

If you need local help

There are national and local starting points.

The Eldercare Locator, a public service of the U.S. Administration for Community Living, connects older adults and families with local services and can be reached at 1-800-677-1116.

Before you leave this page

Make one small improvement to the plan.

You do not have to solve every caregiving question today. Choose one thing: confirm a backup person, verify a phone number, make sure someone can get inside, or complete the emergency information sheet.
