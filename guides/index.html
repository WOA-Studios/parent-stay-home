Guides

Guides for the moment you realize your parent may need more help.

Start with the question closest to what you are facing today. These guides are designed to help you understand what changed, what may need attention first, and what next step could make the situation less chaotic.

Where to start

You do not have to know every question yet.

Many families arrive here because something has changed: a fall, missed meals, driving concerns, medication confusion, a hospital discharge, or the feeling that Mom or Dad needs more support than before. Begin with the guide that matches the concern in front of you.

Safety note: If your parent may be in immediate danger, may have fallen, may be having a medical emergency, may be unable to get to the phone, or the situation feels urgent, call 911 or the appropriate local emergency service right away.

What are you trying to figure out?

Can My Parent Safely Stay Home?

Use this when: you are worried about falls, wandering, unsafe cooking, medication mistakes, isolation, home hazards, or whether more support is needed.

Services That Can Help an Aging Parent Stay Home

Use this when: you are not sure what kinds of help exist or which service category best matches your parent’s need.

What to Do After a Parent Leaves the Hospital

Use this when: your parent is coming home after a hospital stay and you need to think through meals, rides, medications, appointments, paperwork, and home safety.

What Information Should I Know About My Parent's Finances?

Use this when: bills, benefits, insurance, taxes, accounts, or important financial information are hard to locate or understand.

What to Do When Your Parent Can't Drive

Use this when: driving is no longer safe, appointments are being missed, or your parent needs reliable rides for errands, meals, programs, or medical care.

What If I Do Not Live Nearby?

Use this when: you need a just-in-case plan, local contacts, access information, medical communication authorization notes, and a plan for what to do if you cannot reach your parent.

A simple path

If everything feels urgent, read in this order.

You do not have to read every guide today. Start with the one that helps you take the next right step.

  1. What to Do When Your Parent Starts Needing Help — when you need a calm starting point.
  2. Can My Parent Safely Stay Home? — when safety is the main concern.
  3. Services That Can Help an Aging Parent Stay Home — when you need to understand support options.
  4. Get Organized tools — when you need forms, checklists, and family planning sheets.
  5. Find Help directory — when you are ready to search verified resources and providers.

General information

Use these guides as a starting point, not a substitute for professional advice.

Parent Stay Home provides practical information for organization and planning. It is not medical, legal, financial, or care-management advice.

For decisions about healthcare, legal documents, benefits, taxes, accounts, medications, or safety concerns, contact the appropriate qualified professional, agency, or emergency service.
