What Information Should I Know About My Parent's Finances?

A calm, organization-first guide to bills, benefits, accounts, insurance, taxes, and important money-related information families may need during care planning.

Open financial checklist Open papers checklist

Start here

You do not have to take over everything.

The goal is to understand what exists, what may need attention, and who is authorized to help. Start with basic information before there is an urgent bill, missed payment, insurance question, or benefits issue.

  • Know where key financial papers are kept.
  • Understand which bills are paid regularly and how.
  • Identify trusted professionals and authorized contacts.
  • Write down questions that need a qualified answer.
General information note: This guide is for organization and planning only. It is not financial, legal, tax, benefits, medical, or care-management advice. For decisions about accounts, taxes, legal documents, benefits, insurance, Medicaid, Medicare, Social Security, Veterans benefits, or financial authority, contact the appropriate qualified professional, agency, or emergency resource.

Bills and regular payments

Know what must be paid and when.

Missed bills can create stress quickly, especially if mail piles up, automatic payments fail, or no one knows which accounts exist.

Common bills to identify

  • Mortgage, rent, condo fee, or lot rent
  • Electric, gas, water, sewer, trash, phone, internet, and cable
  • Insurance premiums
  • Credit cards, loans, medical bills, and payment plans
  • Home care, transportation, meal delivery, or other support services

Helpful details to gather

  • Company or provider name
  • Account number or customer ID, if available
  • Due date and payment method
  • Whether autopay or paper billing is used
  • Who is authorized to speak with the company

Income, benefits, and support programs

Write down what comes in and what programs may be involved.

You may not need dollar amounts right away. Even knowing which benefits, pensions, or support programs exist can help families ask better questions.

Income sources

Social Security, pension, retirement income, annuity, employment income, rental income, family support, or other regular deposits.

Benefits and programs

Medicare, Medicaid, Veterans benefits, SNAP, energy assistance, property tax relief, senior programs, or county/state support.

Who can answer questions?

Benefits office, insurance plan, Area Agency on Aging, Veterans service officer, tax professional, attorney, financial professional, or case worker.

Accounts and access

Know what exists without assuming you can access it.

Being an adult child does not automatically create legal authority over a parent’s accounts or finances. Permissions, powers of attorney, account authorizations, and professional guidance may matter.

  • List banks, credit unions, investment firms, retirement accounts, and credit cards.
  • Ask whether a trusted person is already authorized on any accounts.
  • Locate legal documents such as financial power of attorney, if one exists.
  • Do not move money, close accounts, change beneficiaries, or sign documents unless proper authority and professional guidance are clear.
Access note: Keep account details private and share them only with people your parent trusts or has legally authorized, when appropriate. If there are concerns about exploitation, missing money, unpaid bills, or urgent financial harm, contact the appropriate qualified professional, agency, or emergency resource.

Insurance and taxes

Some questions need the right professional.

Insurance information

  • Health insurance cards and plan contacts
  • Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medigap, Medicaid, or VA coverage information
  • Prescription drug plan
  • Homeowners, renters, auto, life, or long-term care insurance
  • Policy numbers, agent names, and claim contact information

Tax and property information

  • Most recent tax return location
  • Tax preparer or accountant contact
  • Property tax bill or assessment notice
  • Mortgage, deed, lease, or title documents
  • Important deadlines that should not be missed

Warning signs

Know when money concerns may need urgent attention.

  • Bills are unpaid or utilities may be shut off.
  • Mail is piling up or important notices are being missed.
  • Money, checks, cards, or valuables appear to be missing.
  • Someone is pressuring your parent for money, access, signatures, or gifts.
  • Your parent seems confused by bills, scams, accounts, or payment requests.
  • Housing, benefits, food, medical care, or safety may be affected.
Protection note: If exploitation is suspected, housing may be at risk, benefits may be interrupted, or an urgent financial deadline is near, contact the appropriate qualified professional, agency, adult protective services resource, legal aid resource, or emergency service right away.
