Start Here Guide

What to Do When Your Parent Starts Needing Help

When something changes with a parent, it can feel like every question arrives at once. Start with what feels most urgent, gather what you can, and take one next step.

Open emergency sheet Find local help

You do not have to solve everything today.

This guide is for the moment when you know your parent may need more support, but you are not sure what to do first. The goal is not perfection. The goal is to make the next hour, phone call, or decision a little less chaotic.

Important: If your parent may be in immediate danger, may have fallen, may be having a medical emergency, may be unable to get to the phone, or the situation feels urgent, call 911 or the appropriate local emergency service right away.

Step 1

Write down what changed.

Before you start calling everyone at once, name what is different. This helps you explain the situation clearly and decide what kind of help to look for first.

Safety changed

Falls, trouble with stairs, unsafe cooking, poor lighting, clutter, bathroom concerns, wandering, or not answering the phone.

Daily care changed

Missed meals, hygiene changes, laundry piling up, medication confusion, missed appointments, or difficulty getting around.

Transportation changed

Your parent no longer drives, should not drive, missed appointments, or needs help getting to errands, programs, or medical visits.

Paperwork or money changed

Unpaid bills, lost documents, insurance confusion, benefits questions, tax concerns, or not knowing who has authority to help.

Step 2

Decide what is most urgent.

Use this quick order if everything feels important.

  1. Immediate danger or medical emergency: Call emergency services.
  2. Food, medication, or safe access: Address what affects today or tomorrow first.
  3. Home safety: Look for fall risks, bathroom hazards, stairs, lighting, and access concerns.
  4. Essential information: Gather emergency contacts, doctors, medications, insurance, and key documents.
  5. Ongoing support: Look for local transportation, meals, home safety help, aging resources, or move support.

Step 5

Make one next call or task.

Once you know the most urgent concern, choose one action. One call. One form. One checklist. One conversation. This keeps the situation from becoming a pile of unfinished panic.

  • Call one agency or provider.
  • Complete one emergency information section.
  • Ask one doctor’s office what authorization forms are needed.
  • Ask one trusted local person if they can be an emergency contact.
  • Schedule one safety repair or home review.

Questions to ask before calling

  • Does this organization serve my parent’s county or neighborhood?
  • Who is eligible?
  • Is there a cost, donation, or sliding scale?
  • How soon can help start?
  • What information should I have ready?
  • Is there a waiting list?

Trust and safety

Know when staying home may need more support.

Parent Stay Home is about helping a parent remain home for as long as reasonably possible — not at all costs. If your parent is unsafe at home, repeatedly falling, missing medications, wandering, unable to get food, being exploited, unable to respond in emergencies, or if caregiver support is no longer enough, it may be time to speak with a qualified professional, local aging agency, healthcare provider, or emergency resource about additional options.

General information note: This guide is for organization and planning only. It is not medical, legal, financial, or care-management advice. For decisions about healthcare, legal documents, benefits, taxes, accounts, medications, or safety concerns, contact the appropriate qualified professional, agency, or emergency service.
