Important Papers Checklist for Aging Parents

Know where the most important documents are before you urgently need them.

Download blank PDF

Start with what you can find.

You do not need to gather everything in one sitting. Use this checklist to identify what exists, where it is kept, and who can access it if your parent needs help.

  • Keep the completed checklist somewhere secure and easy to find.
  • Share only with people your parent trusts or has authorized.
  • Review after moves, health changes, account changes, or major family updates.
Privacy note: This checklist is for your personal/family use. Parent Stay Home does not receive, save, store, or see anything you type here. Use the download or print button to keep your own copy.

Legal and decision-making documents

These documents may affect who can help with decisions or access information. Laws and requirements vary, so confirm with an attorney, healthcare provider, or other qualified professional when needed.

Insurance, benefits, and medical information

Use this section to locate cards, letters, portals, and contacts. Do not cancel, change, or apply for benefits based only on a checklist; verify current rules with the relevant agency or qualified advisor.

Financial and household records

This section is meant to help families locate information, not take over decisions. Share financial information only with authorized, trusted people. Consider using a secure password manager or other secure storage for sensitive access details.

Digital access and security

Do not type passwords into this page unless you are saving it securely for personal use. A safer approach may be to write where passwords are stored and who is authorized to access them.

Final arrangements and wishes

This can be a tender subject. Use this section to note what is already documented and where information can be found. Share and discuss only in ways that respect your parent's dignity, wishes, and readiness.
