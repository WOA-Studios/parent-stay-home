Who can get there if you cannot?

Long-distance caregiving can feel frightening because the first question is often not what to do, but who can help nearby. Use this checklist to gather names, permissions, backup contacts, and practical details before an urgent call.

  • Start with nearby people, doctors, pharmacy, transportation, meals, and home access.
  • Confirm who is allowed to receive medical updates before there is an urgent need.
  • Use the action tracker to decide what needs attention now, next, or later.

If I can’t reach my parent

Use this section to create a just-in-case plan before there is an urgent moment. You may never need it, but having names, numbers, access details, and escalation steps written down can reduce panic if your parent does not answer.

Urgent safety note: Call 911 or local emergency services right away if you believe your parent may be in immediate danger, may have fallen, may be experiencing a medical emergency, may be unable to get to the phone, or if the situation feels urgent. Do not ask a neighbor, friend, or family member to enter a situation that may be unsafe.

Medical communication access

Being an adult child does not automatically mean every doctor's office, hospital, pharmacy, specialist, or insurance plan can freely discuss details with you. Before there is an urgent need, ask what authorization, HIPAA release, portal access, or representative documentation is required.
